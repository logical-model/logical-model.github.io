Application 2: calculating and simulating the racial margin

The adjusted racial margin of victory, M, can come from observed election returns or from a prospective voting scenario. This vignette demonstrates both paths.

Calculate M from observed vote shares

comp_M() accepts the vote shares, on a 0–100 scale, of the top minority and top White candidates.

top_minority <- c(18, 40, 85, 20)
top_white <- c(60, 40, 10, 34)

observed_M <- comp_M(Vm = top_minority, Vw = top_white)
observed_M
#> [1] 29.0 50.0 87.5 43.0

Pass the resulting margins to minorep() with the corresponding electorate shares to make predictions.

Simulate M from voting assumptions

For a prospective scenario, sim_M() derives M from the minority electorate share, coethnic voting, and White crossover voting. coethnic is the share of minority voters expected to support the minority candidate; crossover is the analogous White-voter share. Both are proportions from 0 to 1.

hypothetical_C <- c(40, 50, 60)
simulated_M <- sim_M(
  C = hypothetical_C,
  coethnic = 1,
  crossover = 0.3
)
simulated_M
#> [1] 58 65 72

round(minorep(M = simulated_M, C = hypothetical_C), digits = 4)
#> [1] 0.0334 1.0000 1.0000

These assumptions should be explicit and substantively defensible. Varying them is often a useful way to understand how a scenario depends on voting behavior.
