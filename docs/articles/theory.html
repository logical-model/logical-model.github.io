Theory: the logical model of minority representation

The logical model formalizes a simple claim: minority representation requires a minority candidate to emerge and to win. Those outcomes reflect both a district's prior racial electoral environment and the minority group's share of the electorate.

Pr(minority candidate emerges and wins) = Φ(√(MC) − 50)

Here, Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function; M is the adjusted racial margin of victory; and C is the minority share of the electorate. Both are expressed from 0 to 100.

The adjusted racial margin of victory

If Vm and Vw are the vote shares of the top minority and White candidates, respectively, then M = (Vm - Vw) / 2 + 50. Use comp_M() when vote shares are observed and sim_M() for prospective scenarios.

Interpreting predictions

A prediction is conditional on the supplied inputs and assumptions, not a deterministic forecast. The published article evaluates the model using Louisiana mayoral and state legislative elections; its results are not a universal accuracy guarantee for future elections or district plans.
